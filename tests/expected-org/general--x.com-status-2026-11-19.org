corollary: if your data is stored in a database that a company can freely read and access (i.e. not end-to-end encrypted), it's only one breach away

cf. Notion breach

[[https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HW3fUAvbAAAAhfJ?format=webp&name=large][We’re on Enterprise. Notion promises and advertises zero data retention with its AI providers. We still got the Fireworks notice, and I checked the export. It had both sides of the conversation, in detail. The amount of substantive information in there is insane. Notion says it used Fireworks to categorise conversations for “Insights.” Risking private Enterprise chats, after promising zero retention, so you can put those chats into categories for a usage dashboard is one of the stupidest calls I’ve seen in cybersecurity. And they’re trying to soften the announcement by calling the chats “unattributable.” Everyone knows that details about your org, what you're doing, and your clients, etc... end up in the conversation itself. The whole point of Enterprise zero retention is to actually fucking protect this data. The exposure is far more serious than Notion’s wording makes it sound. It’s a massive disgrace, it deserves far more publicity than it’s getting, and I honestly don’t know how]]
